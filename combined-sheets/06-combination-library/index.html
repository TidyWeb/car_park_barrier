Exercise 01

Five books have come back to the library counter. Each one is a dictionary, and all five sit inside a list.

Given

books = [
    {"title": "Tinker Tailor", "days_late": 3, "reference": False},
    {"title": "Gray's Anatomy", "days_late": 2, "reference": True},
    {"title": "The Bell Jar", "days_late": 0, "reference": False},
    {"title": "Kelly's Directory", "days_late": 5, "reference": True},
    {"title": "Wisden 1998", "days_late": 0, "reference": False},
]

Print the title of the third book.

Expected output

The Bell Jar
Stuck?

Two steps, not one. The first bracket picks a book out of the list by its number. The second asks that book for one of its fields by name. Counting starts at nought.

Exercise 02

Reference copies are not supposed to leave the building, so they are treated differently when they come back late.

Reach in for Kelly's Directory and print one sentence or the other, depending on what you find.

Expected output

Kelly's Directory is a reference copy.
Stuck?

The value stored under "reference" is already True or False. An if can test it as it stands — there is nothing to compare it against.

Exercise 03

The fine is 20p for every day a book is late. A reference copy is charged twice that.

Work out what Kelly's Directory owes and print the number on its own. No sentence yet.

Expected output

2.0

Nothing in the data holds the fine. It does not exist until you work it out.

Stuck?

Reach in for the days first and put them in a variable of their own. Then the sum has something short to work with, and the doubling is a separate decision after it.

Exercise 04

The counter needs two numbers at the end of the day: what the shelf owes altogether, and how many books came back with nothing to pay.

Both come from one pass through the books. Print them in a single sentence, with the money to two decimal places.

This is the first formatted-money output in the set. An f-string can show the amount with two digits after the decimal point; :.2f is the format part that asks for that display.

Expected output

The shelf owes £3.40. 2 books were returned with nothing to pay.

The two numbers grow in different ways. One goes up by the fine. The other goes up by one.

Stuck?

Two running records are needed: the shelf total and the count of books with nothing to pay. Give both a neutral starting value before the loop begins.

Still stuck?

For each book, work out its fine first. Then decide whether that result changes the money total, the no-payment count, or both. Keep the single sentence after the loop.
