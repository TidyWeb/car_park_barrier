▸Start with the holding decision

Before code, decide what needs holding together and what you will need to do with it.

The facts

Five customers have a name, a basket of priced items, a loyalty-card fact and a bag count. We must walk through every customer, calculate a bill, and add up the day.

Two structures could hold this. One dictionary keyed by customer name would be useful if the stall repeatedly asked, “What did Marjorie buy?” A list of dictionaries is right here because every job walks every customer in turn. There is no named lookup to do.

That decision is not permanent truth. It is a response to the job in front of us.

→How the shape works

1. Boxes inside boxes

Curly brackets build; square brackets build and reach in. Each customer dictionary keeps facts about one customer together. Their basket is another dictionary inside it: item names point to prices. The outer list is one shelf holding several labelled boxes.

2. The address label

customer["basket"]["apples"] starts at the outside, reaches the basket, then reaches apples. Every bracket is another line of the address.

3. The loop and the carrier

The loop variable is the one customer taken down from the shelf on each pass. basket_total = 0 is a carrier: it exists before prices are added.

4. Independent decisions

Loyalty and free bags are different facts, so they get two ifs. A customer can have both. An elif would silently skip the second question.

5. Output

An f-string joins values into a readable line. :.2f means “always show two digits after the decimal point”, which is what money needs.
